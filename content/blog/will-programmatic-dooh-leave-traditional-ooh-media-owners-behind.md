The future of outdoor advertising will not be decided by who owns the most screens, but by who makes their inventory easiest to discover, buy and measure.

For decades, traditional OOH media owners have built their businesses around premium locations, long-term relationships and direct negotiations. But as advertising moves towards automated planning and buying, a new question is emerging: what happens to media owners whose inventory is not ready for programmatic access?

This is no longer just a technology question. It is a question of future competitiveness.

In September 2026, the Out of Home Advertising Association of America (OAAA) launched its Programmatic & Automation Center of Excellence. In October, it followed with a Technical Advisory Council covering static and digital inventory, direct and programmatic transactions, data standards, APIs and reporting.

The important detail is that these initiatives recognise a challenge across the wider OOH ecosystem, not just digital screens. Their proposed standards and frameworks are still being developed; they are not yet proof that the industry has solved the problem.

The gap is bigger than digital versus static

A static billboard can still offer an excellent location, strong visibility and value for a brand. The challenge is that if its availability, specifications, audience estimates and pricing cannot be easily discovered through modern planning systems, it may be harder for agencies to evaluate alongside other media options.

That does not mean static OOH is becoming irrelevant. It means that valuable inventory can lose commercial visibility when the information around it is difficult to access.

The demand for better evidence is already clear. According to IAB Australia’s 2026 Programmatic DOOH State of the Nation report, three in four surveyed agencies expected programmatic DOOH investment to increase over the following year. Yet 42% identified proving ROI as the biggest barrier to greater investment.

These findings relate to Australian agency respondents, not the entire global market. Still, they highlight an important issue: making inventory available programmatically is only part of the job. Buyers also want reliable delivery reporting, comparable audience metrics and evidence of business outcomes.

Who is moving in the right direction?

Companies are addressing different parts of this challenge. Broadsign provides digital signage and programmatic advertising infrastructure, including Broadsign Reach and Place Exchange. VIOOH, developed by JCDecaux, offers programmatic trading technology and inventory management tools for digital media owners. Both illustrate how technology can connect digital inventory with advertising demand.

However, these examples should not be mistaken for a complete solution for traditional static operators. Static inventory also needs better data management, standardised specifications and integration into planning and sales workflows.

The opportunity is not necessarily for every media owner to build its own technology platform. It is to make its inventory accessible through the right partners and systems.

What should traditional media owners do?

Three practical steps could make a difference:

* Digitise inventory information. Maintain accurate location details, dimensions, availability, campaign dates, specifications and pricing.
* Build hybrid selling capabilities. Keep direct sales where relationships and premium placements matter, while exploring technology partnerships that expand digital discovery and buying access.
* Improve measurement transparency. Clearly distinguish booked placements, proof of posting, estimated audience exposure and independently verified campaign outcomes.

Technology providers should also make integrations easier and more affordable for smaller media owners. Industry bodies can help by establishing common data definitions and interoperable standards.

The real question for the OOH industry

Programmatic buying does not automatically make traditional media owners obsolete. Nor does owning digital screens guarantee commercial success.

The bigger risk is a widening gap between media owners who make their inventory easy to plan, transact and evaluate, and those who remain difficult to integrate into modern media workflows.

The next phase of OOH growth should not be about choosing between static and digital. It should be about connecting valuable physical inventory to the systems through which advertisers make decisions.

Because in the future of outdoor advertising, the most valuable location may not be enough. Advertisers must also be able to find it, understand its value and buy it efficiently.
