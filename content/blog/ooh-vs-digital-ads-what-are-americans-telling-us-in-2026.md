OOH vs. Digital Ads: What Are Americans Telling Us in 2026?

Same ads. Different reactions.

Advertising is everywhere, but people do not perceive every advertising channel in the same way. Two 2026 studies offer an interesting perspective on how Americans view out-of-home (OOH) advertising compared with digital media.

Trust is becoming part of the conversation

Kantar’s The IRL Advantage study surveyed 2,000 U.S. consumers aged 18–64 across 11 metropolitan markets. When asked which advertising environment was most likely to carry fake, misleading or AI-generated content, 32% named social media and apps, compared with just 9% for billboards and outdoor advertising.

This measures perceived risk, not the actual prevalence of misleading content. Still, it highlights an important difference in how consumers view these environments.

OOH can lead to action beyond the street

Research published by the Out of Home Advertising Association of America (OAAA), in partnership with The Harris Poll, found that 86% of U.S. adults aged 18–64 said they would take action after seeing a retail OOH ad that interested them.

The reported actions included:

* 45% — Visit the retailer’s website or app.
* 42% — Search for the retailer online.
* 33% — Visit a physical store.

These responses show how OOH can connect a real-world advertising encounter with digital engagement and potential store visits. They represent stated intentions, rather than proof that every exposure leads to a completed action.

The human side of advertising

The same OAAA study found that 69% of respondents said OOH advertising feels more human than online or mobile advertising, while 64% said it feels more culturally relevant.

For me, the interesting takeaway is that OOH should not be evaluated only by how many people might see an advertisement. Its role in connecting brands with real-world environments—and encouraging people to explore a brand across other channels—also deserves attention.

As DOOH and programmatic capabilities continue to develop, the opportunity is to connect physical exposure with measurable outcomes without losing the human context that makes the medium distinctive.

OOH is more than being seen. It is about what happens next.
